# FAQ

**What is MCP?**  
The Model Context Protocol — an open standard that lets AI models call external tools. Add GEOMETRY once and any MCP client (Claude, Cursor, and others) can query the engine mid-conversation.

**Do I need an account to try?**  
No. Paste `https://mcp.geometry.app/mcp` and ask. Plans/keys come later when you want a metered Free plan or paid tier — see [Connect & manage your key](/connect-and-keys).

**Is the data stable?**  
The date spine and its systems are pre-computed and versioned; the same call returns the same answer today, tomorrow, and next year. The beta label covers tooling and quotas, not the data.

**What's cardology?**  
A birth-date-to-playing-card system (Cards of Destiny tradition) with a full relationship/connection layer. GEOMETRY serves a machine-readable version of it.

**Is this astrology? Do you claim it predicts anything?**  
GEOMETRY serves the *data* of traditional symbolic systems — deterministically. What it means is your product's call. Where we test patterns empirically, we publish what we find, including negative results.

**What does it cost?**  
Soft launch: paste the free MCP URL and go (no account). Metered Free / paid plans and portal keys roll out with self-serve billing. Starter pricing target: **$28/mo**.

**Where's my data stored? Do you see my users' questions?**  
GEOMETRY is a read-only reference lookup. Calls carry birth dates or names as inputs, return JSON, and aren't used to build profiles.

**Where is the full contract / raw JSON?**  
Teaching examples live in these docs (trimmed on purpose). On the wire, every successful tool call returns the **complete** JSON (Tool 2: only `birth_date` — no `full=true`). “Full payload” and “raw JSON” mean that object.

**Chat example (Claude Desktop):** ask `give raw json for birthdate 1985-03-15 card` so the agent pastes the tool result instead of summarizing. RAG / HTTP builders already receive the full object in the tool or API response body.

**Which timezone decides when a cultural year starts?**  
It depends on the system, and that is deliberate. Chinese and Vietnamese lunar years use **CST** (UTC+8), Vedic samvatsara uses **IST** (UTC+5:30), Hebrew and Dreamspell are **timezone-neutral** (arithmetic calendars with no astronomical instant to place in a zone), and Persian uses the equinox in **GMT**. Tools take a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` with no time or zone, and no conversion happens at request time. Full table: [Calendar boundaries](/calendar-boundaries).

**Why doesn't your Nowruz match my Persian calendar library?**  
GEOMETRY anchors Persian years to the equinox in GMT. Iran's official Solar Hijri rule uses Tehran local time and rolls to the next day when the equinox falls after Tehran solar noon. The two disagree on roughly a quarter to a third of years — including 2026 (GEOMETRY March 20, Iran March 21). That is a documented convention choice, not a bug. See [Calendar boundaries](/calendar-boundaries).

**How were moon / season / retrograde dates calculated?**  
Offline with the standard **Swiss Ephemeris** Python stack (widely used in production astronomy/astrology), then stored as lookups. Requests do not run a live ephemeris. Each is published as the Gregorian day containing the instant **in GMT**. Source: open-source [**pyswisseph**](https://github.com/astrorigin/pyswisseph) (Python binding to Astrodienst Swiss Ephemeris) — same stack many production systems use; see also [Data coverage](/coverage).

**Your moon phase date is one day off from mine.**  
Usually a timezone difference, not an error. GEOMETRY publishes the GMT day; an instant lands at a random time of day, so a local date differs roughly `offset ÷ 24` of the time — about a third of the time at UTC+8 or UTC−8. Convert on your side for local dates. See [Calendar boundaries](/calendar-boundaries).
